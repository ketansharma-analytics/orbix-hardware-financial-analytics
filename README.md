# orbix-hardware-financial-analytics
 Strategic Financial Planning &amp; Margin Analysis Project.
Net sales reached **6.8× the FY2019 level.** 📈
Gross margin % went the other way. 📉
That was the first signal that caught my attention while analysing **Orbix Hardware (FY2019–21)** during the Codebasics Data Analytics Bootcamp.
The real question wasn't just, *"How fast were sales growing?"*
It was: **"Where did the growth come from, and what happened to profitability?"**
📊 **THE NUMBERS**
• Net Sales: **87.5M → 196.7M → 598.9M**
• FY21 Sales Growth: **+204.5% YoY**
• Gross Margin %: **41.4% → 37.3% → 36.4%**
• Gross Margin decline since FY19: **5.0 percentage points**

🔍 **KEY FINDINGS**

• APAC contributed **57.5%** of cumulative sales across FY19–21.
• Amazon was the largest customer, with **131.8M** in cumulative sales.
• FY21 COGS grew **208.6% YoY**, slightly faster than sales.
• Sales grew rapidly, but the gross margin rate continued to decline.

I then built an **editable scenario model** using FY21 as the base, with volume, pricing and COGS savings as key levers.

🔴 **Downside:** -10% volume, -2% price
**185.6M gross profit** — 32.6M below FY21

🟡 **Realistic:** +10% volume, 1% COGS saving
**244.2M gross profit** — 26.0M above FY21

🟢 **Upside:** +25% volume, +2% price, 3% COGS saving
**302.0M gross profit** — **39.5% gross margin**

*These are scenario outputs based on illustrative assumptions, not forecasts.*

To translate the analysis into action, I used a **feasibility-impact matrix** to prioritise:

➡️ Supplier negotiation to address COGS pressure
➡️ Targeted pricing for key accounts
➡️ APAC sourcing as a longer-term cost initiative
➡️ Deprioritising divestments with limited impact and higher operational risk

💡 **ONE LESSON I TOOK AWAY**

Getting the formula right is one thing. Making sure the number tells the right business story is another.

I initially reported the FY21 margin change as **-2.3%**. Using the underlying unrounded figures, the change was **-0.85 percentage points**, or **-2.3% in relative terms**.

Both describe the change differently. The important part is knowing which measure answers the business question.
